NCERT Class 12 Chapter 11 Core Concepts

1. Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

Radiation consists of discrete energy packets called photons, each with energy $E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$. When a single photon collides with an electron near the metal surface, it transfers its entire energy instantaneously:

$$K_{\text{max}} = \frac{1}{2}m v_{\text{max}}^2 = h\nu - \Phi_0 = e V_0$$

Where $\Phi_0 = h\nu_0$ is the minimum energy (Work Function) required for an electron to escape the surface, and $V_0$ is the stopping potential.

2. Summary of NCERT Experimental Findings

1. Photocurrent & Intensity For $\nu > \nu_0$, photocurrent is directly proportional to intensity. Intensity $\propto$ number of photons per second $\propto$ photoelectrons emitted per second.
2. Stopping Potential & $\nu$ Stopping potential $V_0$ (and $K_{\text{max}}$) increases linearly with frequency $\nu$ above $\nu_0$, but is completely independent of intensity.
3. Threshold Frequency $\nu_0$ Below $\nu_0$, no photoelectrons are emitted no matter how high the intensity or how long the surface is illuminated.

3. Why Classical Wave Theory Failed

  • Kinetic Energy: Wave theory predicts $K_{\text{max}}$ should increase with intensity (greater electric field amplitude). Experimentally, $K_{\text{max}}$ is independent of intensity.
  • Cut-off Frequency: Wave theory allows electrons to accumulate energy over time at any frequency. Experimentally, no emission occurs if $\nu < \nu_0$.
  • Instantaneous Emission: Wave theory predicts a measurable time lag (hours or days) for an atom-sized cross section to absorb enough energy. Experimentally, photoelectrons are ejected in less than $10^{-9}\text{ s}$.
